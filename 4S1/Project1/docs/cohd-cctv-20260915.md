# CoHD-Tiny adaptation on fixed-camera CCTV — 15 September 2026

**The tested customization did not achieve lower false negatives on the held-out CCTV pilot.** It preserved inference speed and reduced some false detections, but missed the same distant people as original CoHD. Keep the original checkpoint as the default; neither custom variant is a validated CCTV upgrade.

## What we actually changed

- **Original:** released CoHD-Tiny, unchanged weights and normal inference. No verifier or extra module.
- **Custom A:** fine-tuned 198,777 parameters in the existing mask embedding, class prediction and hierarchical mask-selection heads. Image/language encoders and the original absence head stayed frozen. No extra inference module.
- **Custom B:** Custom A plus a 278-coefficient learned verifier. Training weights positive examples four times absent examples, with the threshold selected to avoid additional positive rejection on validation.

Training used 53 labeled fixed-camera MOTS20-09 frames plus 270 historical gRefCOCO expressions. Custom A used foreground-weighted cross entropy, recall-weighted Tversky loss, and a preservation loss on the gRefCOCO replay. Four epochs were run; epoch 4 was selected among epochs 1, 2 and 4 under the predeclared validation constraints. This is an adaptation of [published CoHD](https://github.com/RobertLuo1/CoHD), using an established [Tversky loss](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.05721); it is not a claim of a new architecture.

Training alone cached frozen upstream features for efficiency. **Reported deployment timing always processes the full image**, including the encoders.

## Data integrity and scope

The held-out CCTV pilot contains **120 distinct frames from two viewpoints**:

| Evaluation source | Frames | Native ground truth | Prompt |
|---|---:|---|---|
| MOTS20-02, stationary pedestrian scene | 60 | Human instance masks and ignore regions | all people |
| PersonPath22 / VIRAT courtyard, uid_vid_00147 | 60 | Human visible-person boxes | all people |

MOTS training scene 0009 is separate. Validation used 40 annotated PersonPath22 frames from uid_vid_00144 and uid_vid_00149, plus 90 historical gRefCOCO expressions. Visual checks found the two VIRAT validation videos share the same view despite different source IDs, so they were grouped together. No training/validation/test viewpoint or image hash overlaps were found.

Only annotated PersonPath22 keyframes were used. Frame indices, annotation timestamps, resolution and example box alignment were checked. Crowd, background-person and in-vehicle regions were treated as ignore boxes. No generated masks were substituted for human ground truth. [PersonPath22 documentation](https://github.com/amazon-science/tracking-dataset), [source mapping](https://github.com/amazon-science/tracking-dataset/blob/main/readme/external_dataset.md), [MOTS documentation](https://motchallenge.net/data/MOTS/).

These are daytime scenes with distance, small targets, occlusion, shadows and clutter. **Nighttime CCTV and detailed clothing-description CCTV are not validated here.** The data provide no human-authored referring expressions, so CCTV tests use only the explicit all-person prompt. The separate language regression is described below.

This is a small cross-view pilot. Repeated appearances of the same person across frames are person observations, not independent people. Two test viewpoints cannot support a broad statistical claim about all CCTV cameras. No frame-level confidence interval is used to disguise this limitation. PersonPath22 video/annotation use follows its stated noncommercial research license.

## CCTV segmentation result: MOTS20-02

| Metric | Original | Custom A | Custom B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mean union-mask IoU | **65.56%** | 65.50% | 65.50% |
| Aggregate intersection/union | **67.59%** | 67.55% | 67.55% |
| Target mask coverage recall at 50% | **61.23%** | 60.66% | 60.66% |
| Small-person coverage recall | 31.58% | 31.58% | 31.58% |
| Foreground false-positive pixels | 3,184,222 | **3,170,616** | **3,170,616** |
| Foreground false-negative pixels | **982,724** | 998,058 | 998,058 |

There were 699 annotated person observations, including 19 with visible-mask height below 64 pixels. Coverage recall asks whether the predicted union covers at least half of an individual GT mask. **It is not one-to-one instance detection AP:** merged people can still count as covered. It is accompanied by union IoU and false-positive pixels for this reason. Ignore regions are excluded from pixel scoring.

Custom A slightly reduced excess foreground but also removed correct foreground. The verifier did not change the CCTV masks compared with A.

## CCTV person-localization result: VIRAT courtyard

| Metric | Original | Custom A | Custom B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Matched person observations | 11 / 632 | 11 / 632 | 11 / 632 |
| Missed person observations | 621 | 621 | 621 |
| Recall at box IoU 0.5 | 1.74% | 1.74% | 1.74% |
| False-positive components | 154 | **145** | **145** |
| Precision | 6.67% | **7.05%** | **7.05%** |

CoHD outputs a union mask. For this box-only source, all methods use the same declared conversion: 8-connected components of at least four pixels, component bounding boxes, then one-to-one Hungarian matching to visible-person boxes at IoU >=0.5. Unmatched components primarily inside an ignore box are excluded. These are **mask-to-box localization results**, not official MOT tracking scores or native detector AP. Misses include absent, partial and merged predictions that do not satisfy the matching criterion.

The result is poor for all three variants and must not be summarized as successful CCTV detection. Post-hoc size analysis, for explanation only, found:

| Native visible-person height | Observations | Matched, every variant |
|---|---:|---:|
| Below 32 pixels | 150 | 0 |
| 32–63 pixels | 394 | 2 |
| At least 64 pixels | 88 | 9 |

Fixed-frame visualizations show several distant people without corresponding foreground masks. This head-only training did not recover them. That supports investigating image scale and earlier visual features next; it does not prove that no CoHD training strategy could work.

## Full-image runtime

Twenty-four paired resident requests mixed CCTV and gRefCOCO development images, with rotating method order after warm-up. Every request loaded the image, preprocessed it, ran full CoHD, optionally ran the verifier, and converted the mask to original resolution.

| Method | Mean | Median | p95 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Original | 0.64747 s | 0.64700 s | 0.65489 s |
| Custom A | 0.64743 s | 0.64723 s | 0.65582 s |
| Custom B | 0.64832 s | 0.64778 s | 0.65587 s |

Custom A's time difference is effectively zero (paired overhead CI −0.062% to +0.052%). Custom B adds approximately 0.131% (CI +0.067% to +0.203%). Both pass the <5% overhead requirement. Peak PyTorch allocated GPU memory was 992,915,456 bytes for each mode; this is not total nvidia-smi process memory.

Initial checkpoint loading, experimental weight switching and disk export are excluded. Comparisons are on the same CMP70HX, using the authors' GPU PyTorch deformable-attention fallback. No speed claim is based on cached-head training.

## Language regression status

The previous 300-image gRefCOCO set is used only as a **reused regression diagnostic**, not an untouched test. It was excluded from this round's fitting and checkpoint selection. Attempts to reserve another balanced sample from the available unexamined annotations found too few eligible cases; no failed selection was silently relabeled a complete new test.

| Reused gRefCOCO diagnostic | Original | Custom A | Custom B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Positive mean IoU | 76.43% | 76.42% | 75.08% |
| Absent-target accuracy | 32% | 32% | 62% |
| Positive images returning empty | 1 / 200 | 1 / 200 | 6 / 200 |

A essentially preserved the original language-mask behavior. B again traded positive recall for absence rejection: five additional positive images returned empty masks. Its higher absence accuracy does not establish a low-false-negative upgrade. No new statistical superiority claim is based on this reused sample.

## Decision and evidence

The predeclared goal required fewer missed people, controlled false positives and less than 5% runtime overhead. **Runtime passes; the primary CCTV recall goal fails.** Custom B produces exactly the same masks as A on all 120 CCTV test images. Preserve both as experimental checkpoints and retain original CoHD as the default.

Execution checks passed: all 300 rerun original gRefCOCO masks were pixel-identical to their saved historical baseline, and all 72 integrated predictions checked against cached-head reconstruction were identical (24 images × 3 methods). The mask-to-box scorer passed known-answer checks for matching, misses, false components and ignored regions. Trained checkpoint hashes matched their frozen locks.

Custom A checkpoint SHA-256: `33462549997a4e8c9aab3f8574a00af6492784799b669c7633370989c2045686`. Custom B checkpoint SHA-256: `802b04607a57572e5e3644f57f0d32ffb9faaa4aa0b5da96c5318bc49383b6dd`.

Artifacts are in `code/results/cohd_cctv_20260915/`: source and split records, protocol, validation histories, frozen checkpoint locks, predictions, scores, runtime pairs, and six fixed-frame comparison images with zooms. Examples use the 25%, 50%, and 75% positions of each sampled test sequence; they are not selected for favorable model performance. Full native inputs and training caches remain on cenara70hx.

The remote CLI `code/scripts/infer_cctv_custom.py` accepts `--model original`, `customA`, or `customB`; the default is original. No portal default was changed. The next experimental round would need an independently reserved test and a change capable of improving small-person features, rather than another rejection threshold selected from these test failures.
